## V311 provider-lease/manifest loss trace

The supplied V310 facts (successful manifest preflight for attempt one; attempt two returned `manifest_unavailable` before dispatch while peer state/roster remained authorized) are consistent with the SDK’s separate provider-availability state. `handleProviderLease` rejects stale cursors, and on a fresh unavailable tombstone or TTL expiry `clearRemoteAvailability` clears the lease and manifest and records the lease cursor floor (`packages/aurora-sdk/src/webrtc/mesh-peer-bridge.ts:1200-1210,1243-1268,1303-1322`). Peer authorization is deliberately independent: this changes service availability without closing the authorized WebRTC session. A later fresh lease is accepted only against the cursor floor (same epoch with higher revision or a new epoch); it marks availability active, and a fresh manifest/lease handshake can repopulate the manifest (`:1116-1164,1200-1217,1243-1249`). On the provider, resume starts a new lease epoch and sends a manifest; inbound service admission and its new lease follow a matching manifest ACK (`packages/aurora-sdk/src/peer-host/webrtc-peer-host.ts:232-282,313-338`).

`getManifest` intentionally returns `null` immediately when `remoteAvailability === 'unavailable'` (`mesh-peer-bridge.ts:422-435`); it does not wait for future recovery. Existing tests explicitly assert immediate null after provider-unavailable and lease TTL (`packages/aurora-sdk/tests/webrtc-mesh-peer-bridge.test.ts:1150-1190,1192-1235`), and the fresh-lease generation test verifies stale tombstones/leases cannot override a newer valid lease (`:1717-1792`). Thus the current second preflight’s zero-time `manifest_unavailable` is a fail-closed availability result, not evidence that the authenticated peer was lost. It could mean a still-active tombstone/expired lease at the instant of the call; the source does not distinguish which occurred in V310. The SDK has a recovery path once valid new protocol frames arrive, but the one-shot preflight will not wait if called during the unavailable interval. No SDK bug or safe policy-preserving repair is established from the available evidence; do not bypass unavailable state, accept stale leases, or alter authorization. Backend lease withdrawal cause remains with the backend owner.
